Act as an expert Named Entity Recognizer (NER) with a critical focus on precision, classification, and alignment with specified categories. Your task is to **extract ALL entities** from the provided text that match EACH **explicitly specified category**—items not matching all specified categories (or not in categories at all) must be omitted.

For each entity, classify its `presence_type` as exactly one of:

1. **literal_entity**: A character or entity physically present in the scene, taking action, speaking, or explicitly existing within the diegetic reality. They are "in the room."
2. **mentioned_entity**: A real character in the story universe who is only spoken about, referred to, or thought about in this scene. They exist as a person within the story world but are not physically present.
3. **allusive_figure**: A metaphorical, historical, philosophical, or mythological figure invoked purely as a rhetorical device, comparison, or quotation source. Not a character in the story's reality.

## Examples

literal_entity:

- "Elena drew her sword and stepped forward." → Elena (present, acting)
- "Bram sat by the fire, humming to himself." → Bram (present, described)
- "Kael said nothing. His jaw was tight." → Kael (present, speaking/non-verbal communication)

mentioned_entity:

- "We must find Marcus before the storm hits." → Marcus (spoken about, not present in scene)
- "She wondered if Lira had made it to the coast." → Lira (thought about, not in scene)
- "The captain left orders from General Draven." → Draven (referenced via orders, not present)

allusive_figure:

- "He bore his burden like Sisyphus." → Sisyphus (mythological comparison, not in story)
- "A regular Napoleon, conquering every argument." → Napoleon (historical metaphor)
- "Her resolve was Promethean—burning bright against the dark." → Prometheus (mythological rhetorical device)

## Rules

- **Entity Names**: Use concise, unambiguous names grounded in the text, no more than 200 characters per name. Names may exceed two words when necessary for identity, within that character limit. Do not invent names for unnamed entities.
- **Entity Structure**: Each extracted entity has a `name` (the entity string) and a `presence_type` (one of the three values above).
- **Category Fidelity**: Include *only* entities from categories *explicitly listed* at the start of the prompt. Omit all other potential entities, even if the text strongly implies they fit.
- **Character Entities**: Use names as given in the text. Omit incidental honorifics when the remaining name identifies the character unambiguously. Prefer names or descriptive identifiers that remain meaningful without a leading honorific (e.g., "Keeper of the Dawn Archive"). Include all unique characters; avoid duplicate aliases unless explicitly noted.
- **Location Entities**: At extraction, retain meaningful modifiers and possessives in concise names (e.g., "Eastern Courtyard" and "Western Courtyard", or "Kalia's childhood home"). Omit incidental surrounding prose. Later normalization and alias deduplication apply separately; this extraction instruction does not guarantee that every descriptive distinction survives those stages.
- **Additional Categories** (e.g., company, date, organization): Apply tailored rules *exactly* (e.g., companies as unmodified brand names; dates formatted as "day-month-year" if context allows, else "year" if day/month is unspecified).
- **Non-character categories** (locations, objects, dates, organizations): Default `presence_type` to `literal_entity` unless the entity is invoked purely as an allusion or reference.
- **When uncertain**: If the entity is physically in the scene → `literal_entity`. If it is a story-world entity only talked about → `mentioned_entity`. If it is invoked for rhetorical effect with no story-world presence → `allusive_figure`.

All rules are enforced—no explanations, context, or extra text allowed.
